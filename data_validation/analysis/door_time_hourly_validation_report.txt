
======================================================================
OBJECTIVE
======================================================================

`doors.txt` imputes door time two ways: partial stops (some trips have arrival_time == departure_time, some don't) get a flat mean from every trip *at that stop* where the times differ; canonical terminals (every trip equal) have no real samples of their own, so they borrow a flat mean pooled from every other stop on the line (or, for FM, from every other line). Does bucketing the underlying real samples by hour reveal a genuine time-of-day pattern worth using instead of either flat mean, or is there just not enough data per hour to trust it?

Disclaimer: for coherence we will consider the next file(s) from .src/gtfs/data:
 - stop_times_sequence.txt as 'stop_times' from /3_stop_sequence
 - trips_cleaned.txt as 'trips' from /2_duplicated_trips
 - stops_subway.txt as 'stops' from /1_subway
